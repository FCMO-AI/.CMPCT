## Engineering law

Every material task is governed by both:

1. the FCMO AGI Engineering & Operations Standard v1.0 as universal parent behavior; and
2. `docs/AGI_ENGINEERING_STANDARD.md` as CMPCT's specialized engineering and promotion discipline.

The local quality ratchet remains binding. In particular:

- establish the problem/opportunity and direct baseline before implementation;
- identify the mechanism and state a falsifiable hypothesis/disproof condition;
- consider meaningfully different solution classes for non-trivial work;
- preserve losses, ambiguity and rejected experiments;
- measure exported costs such as bytes decoded, memory, dependency depth, startup cost and reader complexity;
- use independent or adversarial evidence proportionally to consequence;
- do not weaken a gate to make a candidate pass;
- do not promote a research representation merely because it wins one benchmark.

## Current development horizon

Active future work may explore v0.30 or later mechanisms on non-canonical branches. Those branches are **not current release authority** merely because they are newer or more ambitious. Their evidence should be evaluated against the direct current/base contract and merged only when the relevant repository gates are actually satisfied.

A new agent should therefore begin from `main`, the v0.29.0 release note, the current roadmap and the relevant active branch/PR evidence—not from an old campaign status sentence.

## CI and evidence topology

Ordinary validation, native correctness, performance-release evidence and deep historical research are distinct evidence lanes. Historical experiment workflows may remain manually reproducible after their conclusions are durably recorded rather than waking on every unrelated pull request.

A queued job is not a pass. A workflow that fails before executing the relevant test does not prove the product failed. Conversely, a real benchmark regression must not be wished away because the code change appears unrelated; establish causal relevance or repair the evidence topology.
