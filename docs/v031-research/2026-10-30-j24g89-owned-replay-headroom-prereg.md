**Frozen:** 2026-10-30 UTC; research O0 only. This is an independent evidence lane, not the owner of #823 Reader, #795 signed replay, #836 Writer, or #842 ordinal checkpoint; no product/format/release/benchmark/policy mutation.

## Authority and baseline
Published main is `80c358f8824ec1b20d349c92ed94c898090969b0` (v0.30.0, revision 24). #809 already demonstrated that **original** replay explains ~97–98% of the excess cost of a 63-delete journal versus a checkpoint; #823 subsequently measured a *different* fresh-private-owned-decoder Reader at 14.082 ms / 79.887 ms on 8,192/32,768 genuine journal objects on Ubuntu. Those historical measurements are not this run's timings. #838 and #839 failed full/factorized checkpoint O0 bytes budgets, while #842's ordinal/checkpoint O0 passed its byte gate but was slower than a simpler deleted-ordinal control. It is unresolved whether deleting replay even matters once one-pass reader ownership eliminates the repeated O(N) work.

## One primary question and alternatives
What fraction of a **correctly parsed, source-authenticated real 63-delta index reconstruction** remains on the single-pass *logical replay* path, versus base-index Zstandard/MessagePack decoding and compulsory index materialization? An adversarially generous **free replay** oracle is used only as an upper bound; it cannot earn product credit or prove a checkpoint useful.

Compare (A) original authentic signed r24 63 one-operation DELETE chain, decoded and processed by an independent one-pass ordinal set -> filtered list; (B) a gift of zero replay work after reading exactly the same physical bytes and authenticating exactly the same 63 signed payloads, with unchanged full materialization; (C) baseline checkpoint64 full-index decompression, measured separately rather than conflated with A/B. A real K32 hypothetical checkpoint would still carry extra bytes, references, materialization, cryptographic work and Reader grammar changes, so B is deliberately stronger than any real new-format option in the **replay-work-only** scope.

## Prediction and kill criterion (preregistered before instrument/result)
Prediction H1: under equal predecoded base and signed deltas, the one-pass survivor-operation CPU/wall share of complete offline parse/decode/materialization cost is **<10%** in both N=8,192 and N=32,768, and removing replay work cannot earn a >=10% end-to-end warm-open gain. If both <10%, retire *checkpoint primarily to eliminate DELETE replay* as a high-leverage productization path for these two regimes, while preserving wider workloads as unmeasured; 10–20% is ambiguous and calls for source-exact Reader attribution; >=20% on either size justifies a stronger source-exact product court. Do not move thresholds after seeing samples.

## Instrument and source-of-truth discipline
Use system libzstd via ctypes and Python MessagePack, no cmpct import. Verify original header SHA, 65 linked signed footers, 63 expected authentic DELETEs, archive SHA, canonical final names and checkpoint64 equivalence. Measure each phase with `time.perf_counter_ns` and `process_time_ns` on repeated warm samples with shuffled/permuted ordering; keep raw samples and median, highlight clock/environment and dependent-stage attribution limitations. Independently compare recomputed final index and tampered footer rejection. Estimate operation-only replay share and upper-bound Amdahl speed from complete cold-neutral decoder wall, but **never call a simulated speedup a product speedup**. Count existing bytes + contemplated K32 delta premium from #842, new index memory and future Reader complexity qualitatively.

## Stronger control, failure modes, concurrency
#823 owns Reader algorithm and #842 owns checkpoint descriptor. This lane never writes those surfaces. Unchanged original signed deltas are the strong simple control. Plausible alternative: Python prototype's index materialization dominates due to language overhead, or footer validation dominates independent of replay; neither makes the oracle a shipping recommendation. Fail closed on malformed signed physical objects, unexpected op shape, incorrect checkpoint index, footer SHA, mismatched physical bytes, inability to parse source, implausible clocks or hidden provenance. Negative results are retained.
