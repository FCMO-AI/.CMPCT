## Decision and mechanism

**Primary question:** Is bounded *reuse-informed* admission (2Q family: small FIFO cold queue, protected LRU for observed second touches, bounded ghost of recently evicted cold keys) a credible alternative to O169's 64-MiB LRU on O175-style scans *without* the phase-shift fragility that falsified Guarded 48/16/128 in O188b?

The experiment is a deterministic stdlib-only policy simulator; any retained hits are **not** actual Reader CPU, RSS, wall, throughput or release credit. We deliberately keep the existing 64-MiB **total charged residency** budget, 32768 active-or-ghost key limit, and charge modeled metadata (128 bytes per resident/ghost key) rather than comparing an uncharged 2Q ghost against charged LRU. This is a declared *modeled* key charge, not measured Python heap accounting. Both 2Q and simpler controls receive it consistently.

2Q is a different causal mechanism from static protected-first-fill and reset thresholds: it changes admission after evidence of reuse, with bounded recent eviction identity (ghost queue). Candidate partition is declared before execution: at most 16 MiB in cold FIFO; protected LRU occupies the rest of the 64 MiB charged budget; ghost names bounded by 64 or the remaining entry budget, whichever smaller. Cold entries promote on a second touch; ghost hits admit to protected queue. Oversized entries bypass caching. Never retain decoded byte payload in simulation.

## Baselines, alternative explanations and controls

- Canonical O169 64-MiB LRU (strong simple adaptive baseline).
- O188 fixed-pin first-fit (strong simple repeated-scan control; known failure under shift).
- O188 Guarded 48/16/128 (negative reference; **not** retuned).
- NoCache (minimum retained-residency control).
- The 2Q candidate and a simple two-touch admission control without protected partition, if implementable without confounding.

The candidate might appear to win only because ghost metadata is free, because the scan order matches its eviction order, because a phase label leaks into policy, because its cold queue is secretly an unbounded cache, or because modeled hits poorly predict expensive ZIP reconstruction. Audit all of these before claiming decision value.

## Frozen discriminators before observing results

Run each policy with the same request stream, exact key/size and phase labels solely for external reporting. All policies observe only current key/size and their own causal history. Include:

1. Three ascending scans of 96 distinct 1MiB objects; second-pass reuse (O175-like).
2. One ascending scan96 followed by a **descending** scan96 of identical keys (O188b reversal).
3. A96 twice then fresh B48 for at least four passes; B2 early adaptation (O188b hostile).
4. A fit48 repeatedly accessed stable working set.
5. 16 hot 1MiB keys interleaved with new 96MiB scan streams.
6. An 8×4MiB repeatedly used VZIP-proxy set and fresh 1MiB RAW streams; report *separately* hit counts and arbitrary refetch proxy weights (never CPU).
7. Cold unique entries and zero-byte 40,000-key pressure; assert both byte and entry/ghost bounds.

**Prospective decision thresholds (not product or release gates):** for 2Q to remain a plausible general cache-admission research seed, second-forward scan96 must produce >=32 hits where LRU has 0; reverse scan must retain >=56 hits where LRU has 64; B48 second post-shift pass must retain >=40 hits where LRU has 48; fit48 second pass must produce all 48 hits; no budget violation/semantic difference allowed. These intentionally demanding joint tests may falsify the 2Q candidate. A passing simulation only permits a *new*, independently preregistered real-Reader court (same authenticated r24 O175 archive plus mixed RAW/VZIP and phase shift; repeated same-process read timing/CPU/peak RSS and physical output SHA). The strongest simpler control must remain explicitly visible. If 2Q loses hard on reverse or shift, retire this fixed two-queue configuration rather than threshold-gardening.
